Meet dd-pits

Everything after the timer.

dd-pits sits beside FPVTrackside on the timing laptop, or runs inside RotorHazard as a plugin, and turns the timing data into everything a race director wants on the day. Configure it all in a browser. Store it in one file.

How to use dd-pits

From download
to race day in minutes.

Seven steps, all in the browser. Do the first two and you already have a live dashboard; the rest are optional.

  1. 1

    Download and run

    Download dd-pits and run it on the same laptop as FPVTrackside. The setup guide opens in your browser on first run.

    ./dd-pits
  2. 2

    Switch on FPVTrackside's web server

    It's off by default, and it's how dd-pits reads the timing. In FPVTrackside open Menu → Application Settings, tick Auto start HTTP Server under Web, restart, and open your event. The dashboard lights up; share the laptop's address with pilots over the venue wifi.

    Application Settings → Web → Auto start HTTP Server

    Optional, for faster lap calls: under Gate / LED POST notifications, set the notifier to LegacyMode and NotificationURL to http://localhost:8088/fpvtrackside/events.

  3. 3

    Put it on screens

    Open the TV view on a screen at the pits, and add the overlay to OBS as a Browser Source.

    /live · /overlay
  4. 4

    Choose who speaks

    Pick whether dd-pits or FPVTrackside makes the race calls. dd-pits sets FPVTrackside's speech to match.

    /setup/calls
  5. 5

    Wire up the goggles

    Add each pilot's bind phrase, connect an ELRS netpack or USB backpack, then lay out the goggle screen.

    /setup/pilots · /setup/backpack · /setup/osd
  6. 6

    Turn on commentary

    Pick a writer and a voice. Hear it on the laptop's speakers, or add the audio to OBS as a Media Source.

    /setup/booth · /booth.wav
  7. 7

    After the race

    Paste the stream's YouTube link and place one heat. Every heat gets a Watch link, and you get chapters to paste into the description.

    /setup/video

Ladder

Brackets by seed, several rounds each, ranked on a points table you set. Drop-worst optional.

  • 25 · 18 · 15 · 12 …
  • High or low points win

New Marshalling · RotorHazard timers

Every lap.
Exactly right.

dd-pits reads each pilot's signal from your RotorHazard timer, finds the enter and exit lines for you, and points at the laps that look wrong, with the fix one tap away. Corrections go back through RotorHazard's own marshal save, so results, brackets and FPVTrackside all follow.

dd-pits · Marshal

Lines found for you

Auto-detect measures the background and the passes and picks the enter and exit lines that give steady, believable laps, then tells you how sure it is and why.

Problems pointed out

Double counts, stray signals, missed passes (with the weak peak where it probably was), passes only just over the line, and too few laps for the race.

Bleed spotted

Every pilot on one time axis. A weak bump that lines up with another pilot's strong pass is called out as their signal bleeding across, not a lap.

Drag, tap, done

Drag the lines and the laps recalculate as you go, by RotorHazard's own rule. Tap a pass to drop it, tap the trace to add one. From the laptop or a phone at the field.

Saved the RotorHazard way

Corrections save through RotorHazard's own marshal save, and Use these lines from now on sets the node for the races to come.

FPVTrackside too

When FPVTrackside times with a RotorHazard, dd-pits finds the timer in FPVTrackside's settings and marshals from its signal. Corrections land back in FPVTrackside.

RotorHazard plugin

Racing on RotorHazard? It's all here too.

Install dd-pits as a RotorHazard plugin and the timer itself runs everything: the phone dashboard with up-next heats and the leaderboard, the TV view, every OBS overlay, race calls, commentary and goggle OSD. No laptop needed beside it.

1 · Upload the plugin

On RotorHazard 4.3 or later, open Settings → Plugins, upload the zip and restart. On 4.0 to 4.2, copy the dd_pits folder into src/server/plugins.

2 · Start it once online

The plugin downloads the right dd-pits for your Pi (32- or 64-bit), checks it against the release checksums, and keeps it running. After that, no internet is needed.

3 · Share the dashboard

Pilots open http://<timer>:8088. RotorHazard's Settings → dd-pits panel links to the dashboard, the overlays and dd-pits' settings, behind your RotorHazard login.

Race calls

One voice.
Right on the lap.

Laps, holeshots, results and "next up" — spoken by dd-pits instead of FPVTrackside's own speech, in your club's own wording.

Your club's wording

Calls are read from FPVTrackside's own Sounds.xml, so the phrasing and on/off switches you set there carry over. dd-pits finds FPVTrackside's files itself, and an audit flags settings that disagree.

One switch decides who speaks

dd-pits sets FPVTrackside's speech volume to match, so you never hear both — or neither. No voice set up? FPVTrackside keeps speaking and dd-pits stays out of the audio.

Fast calls

dd-pits listens for FPVTrackside's live events as well as polling it. Voice packs of pre-generated clips make common calls play instantly.

Race director controls

Hurry Up, Announce Race and the other calls FPVTrackside binds to keyboard shortcuts — trigger them from the laptop or any phone at the field.

Pronunciations

Tell the voice how to say a tricky callsign without changing how it's spelled anywhere else.

Clean hand-offs

When the operator moves to the next race, anything still waiting to be said about the last one is dropped.

New Race-day HDZero firmware

Change channel on the goggles.
The quad follows.

Free custom firmware for HDZero goggles and their ELRS backpack. Pick a channel on the goggles and the drone's VTX, your radio and the race timer all retune with you. And the timer can put every pilot on their race channel, Lowband included.

Goggles drive the quad

Dial to a channel and confirm: the backpack sends it to your drone's VTX, your radio's VTX Administrator and the timer. Turn on Auto Send VTX to follow every change, or long-press to send (and re-send) on demand. A VTX SENT pill confirms it on screen.

Every band, Lowband too

The race director sets a pilot to Raceband or L1–L8 from dd-pits and the goggles switch band and tune. Stock firmware can't take a Lowband channel from the timer. The race-day builds also let the dial reach every band.

DVR named after the heat

dd-pits labels each pilot's next recording as their heat is cued, and the goggles add it to the usual name: hdz_0042-WinterCup-Mains-A-R5-H1.mp4. Find any race in seconds. Turn it on once: Record Option → Naming Scheme → ELRS.

The right time, always

The goggle clock is set from the timer over the backpack, so every recording carries the correct date and time.

Nothing taken away

Stock HDZero firmware with the race-day features added on top. Covers HDZero Goggle, Goggle 2 and BoxPro. The DVR and Lowband changes are submitted upstream to HDZero.

Two files, flashed as a pair

One for the goggles (Goggle, Goggle 2 or BoxPro), one for the goggle backpack.

Questions.

Does it work with RotorHazard?

Yes. Install the dd-pits RotorHazard plugin (RotorHazard 4.0 or later) and the timer runs dd-pits itself, with the same dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggle OSD.

Do brackets work with FPVTrackside?

Yes, with the current alpha version of FPVTrackside, which has the REST API they need. Everything else in dd-pits works with any FPVTrackside. dd-pits creates every race in FPVTrackside with its pilots and channels, starts it, and moves the bracket on when it finishes. The Meeting editor lets you change rounds, races, line-ups and pilots in FPVTrackside from dd-pits too.

Can it fix a missed or double-counted lap?

Yes. With a RotorHazard timer (on its own, or as FPVTrackside's timer), the Marshal page shows each pilot's signal, finds the enter and exit lines for you and flags the laps that look wrong, with the fix one tap away. With other timers you can still remove or add a pilot's laps by hand.

Does it replace FPVTrackside?

No. FPVTrackside keeps doing the timing. dd-pits reads it on the same laptop and does everything else: the dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggles.

Does it need the internet?

No. The dashboard, overlays, race calls, goggle OSD and API all run on the laptop. Only the cloud AI options (Gemini or Qwen) and YouTube Watch links use a connection — and the commentary booth can run fully locally on Apple Silicon.

How do pilots see the dashboard?

They open the laptop's address on their phones over the venue wifi. Settings pages only accept changes from the laptop itself, so a phone in the pits can't change anyone's goggle channel.

What does it cost?

Nothing. dd-pits is MIT licensed. If you use cloud AI for commentary, you pay your provider for what you use — and the /booth page shows the running spend.

Can pilots change channel from their HDZero goggles?

Yes, with the free race-day HDZero firmware: change channel on the goggles and the drone's VTX, the radio and the timer follow. It also lets the race director put pilots on Lowband channels and names DVR recordings after the heat.

Do I need an ELRS backpack?

Only for goggle features. A netpack on the network is fastest and confirms delivery; a USB backpack plugged into the laptop works too.

What if no voice is set up?

FPVTrackside keeps announcing exactly as it always has, and dd-pits stays out of the audio. You can never end up with silence by accident.
