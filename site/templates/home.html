Meet dd-pits

Everything after the timer.

dd-pits sits beside FPVTrackside on the timing laptop, or runs inside RotorHazard as a plugin, and turns the timing data into everything a race director wants on the day. Configure it all in a browser. Store it in one file.

Why it works

One race.
Everything at once.

No single feature makes race day easy. It's that every part of dd-pits reads the same timing and acts together, so one action from the race director sets off everything below. Here's one race.

1 · The race is cued

  • Every pilot's goggles switch to their race channel, Lowband too
  • Their DVR is told to name the recording after the heat
  • The dashboard on every phone shows who's up
  • The stream walks the line-up on with ranks and best laps
  • Race calls announce who's up next

2 · They race

  • Each pilot sees their position and lap times in their goggles
  • Live times on the dashboard and on the TV in the pits
  • Overlays update on stream, lap by lap
  • The AI commentary booth calls the action
  • Lap calls in one voice, in your club's wording

3 · It finishes

  • Results and every pilot's personal bests, on every phone
  • The bracket moves on and the next races go to the timer
  • Any lap that looks wrong is flagged on the Marshal page
  • The podium and standings on stream
  • The next race is up, and it all starts again

All of it from one laptop or the timer itself, with RotorHazard or FPVTrackside. No internet, no account.

Works with your timer

RotorHazard or FPVTrackside.

dd-pits works with both. Here's how it runs with each, and what you need.

RotorHazard 4.0 or later

Runs on the timer itself

Install the dd-pits plugin and RotorHazard runs dd-pits for you. Nothing else to install: open it from RotorHazard's menu.

  • Live dashboard and OBS overlays
  • Race calls and AI commentary
  • Lap times in pilots' goggles
  • Brackets: each race becomes a heat, and results come back by themselves
  • Marshal from each pilot's signal: lines found for you, odd laps flagged

Heats, classes and pilots stay in RotorHazard, where you already manage them.

Download the RotorHazard plugin

FPVTrackside

Runs beside it on the laptop

Download dd-pits and run it next to FPVTrackside. Switch on FPVTrackside's web server and dd-pits reads the timing.

With any FPVTrackside

  • Live dashboard and OBS overlays
  • Race calls and AI commentary
  • Lap times in pilots' goggles
  • Signal marshalling when it times with a RotorHazard

Alpha With the FPVTrackside alpha

  • Brackets: races created in FPVTrackside, results come back by themselves
  • Meeting editor: rounds, races, line-ups and pilots from any browser
  • Start races and fix laps from dd-pits

For now these need the alpha version of FPVTrackside. They use FPVTrackside's new REST API, which is only in the current alpha. Once it's in a normal release, any up-to-date FPVTrackside will do.

Questions.

Does it work with RotorHazard?

Yes. Install the dd-pits RotorHazard plugin (RotorHazard 4.0 or later) and the timer runs dd-pits itself, with the same dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggle OSD.

Do brackets work with FPVTrackside?

Yes, with the current alpha version of FPVTrackside, which has the REST API they need. Everything else in dd-pits works with any FPVTrackside. dd-pits creates every race in FPVTrackside with its pilots and channels, starts it, and moves the bracket on when it finishes. The Meeting editor lets you change rounds, races, line-ups and pilots in FPVTrackside from dd-pits too.

Can it fix a missed or double-counted lap?

Yes. With a RotorHazard timer (on its own, or as FPVTrackside's timer), the Marshal page shows each pilot's signal, finds the enter and exit lines for you and flags the laps that look wrong, with the fix one tap away. With other timers you can still remove or add a pilot's laps by hand.

Does it replace FPVTrackside?

No. FPVTrackside keeps doing the timing. dd-pits reads it on the same laptop and does everything else: the dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggles.

Does it need the internet?

No. The dashboard, overlays, race calls, goggle OSD and API all run on the laptop. Only the cloud AI options (Gemini or Qwen) and YouTube Watch links use a connection — and the commentary booth can run fully locally on Apple Silicon.

How do pilots see the dashboard?

They open the laptop's address on their phones over the venue wifi. Settings pages only accept changes from the laptop itself, so a phone in the pits can't change anyone's goggle channel.

What does it cost?

Nothing. dd-pits is MIT licensed. If you use cloud AI for commentary, you pay your provider for what you use — and the /booth page shows the running spend.

Can pilots change channel from their HDZero goggles?

Yes, with the free race-day HDZero firmware: change channel on the goggles and the drone's VTX, the radio and the timer follow. It also lets the race director put pilots on Lowband channels and names DVR recordings after the heat.

Do I need an ELRS backpack?

Only for goggle features. A netpack on the network is fastest and confirms delivery; a USB backpack plugged into the laptop works too.

What if no voice is set up?

FPVTrackside keeps announcing exactly as it always has, and dd-pits stays out of the audio. You can never end up with silence by accident.
