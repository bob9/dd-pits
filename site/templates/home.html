Meet dd-pits

Everything after the timer.

How to use dd-pits

From download
to race day in minutes.

Seven steps, all in the browser. Do the first two and you already have a live dashboard; the rest are optional.

  1. 1

    Download and run

    Download dd-pits and run it on the same laptop as FPVTrackside. The setup guide opens in your browser on first run.

    ./dd-pits
  2. 2

    Switch on FPVTrackside's web server

    It's off by default, and it's how dd-pits reads the timing. In FPVTrackside open Menu → Application Settings, tick Auto start HTTP Server under Web, restart, and open your event. The dashboard lights up; share the laptop's address with pilots over the venue wifi.

    Application Settings → Web → Auto start HTTP Server

    Optional, for faster lap calls: under Gate / LED POST notifications, set the notifier to LegacyMode and NotificationURL to http://localhost:8088/fpvtrackside/events.

  3. 3

    Put it on screens

    Open the TV view on a screen at the pits, and add the overlay to OBS as a Browser Source.

    /live · /overlay
  4. 4

    Choose who speaks

    Pick whether dd-pits or FPVTrackside makes the race calls. dd-pits sets FPVTrackside's speech to match.

    /setup/calls
  5. 5

    Wire up the goggles

    Add each pilot's bind phrase, connect an ELRS netpack or USB backpack, then lay out the goggle screen.

    /setup/pilots · /setup/backpack · /setup/osd
  6. 6

    Turn on commentary

    Pick a writer and a voice. Hear it on the laptop's speakers, or add the audio to OBS as a Media Source.

    /setup/booth · /booth.wav
  7. 7

    After the race

    Paste the stream's YouTube link and place one heat. Every heat gets a Watch link, and you get chapters to paste into the description.

    /setup/video

RotorHazard plugin

Racing on RotorHazard? It's all here too.

Install dd-pits as a RotorHazard plugin and the timer itself runs everything: the phone dashboard with up-next heats and the leaderboard, the TV view, every OBS overlay, race calls, commentary and goggle OSD. No laptop needed beside it.

1 · Upload the plugin

On RotorHazard 4.3 or later, open Settings → Plugins, upload the zip and restart. On 4.0 to 4.2, copy the dd_pits folder into src/server/plugins.

2 · Start it once online

The plugin downloads the right dd-pits for your Pi (32- or 64-bit), checks it against the release checksums, and keeps it running. After that, no internet is needed.

3 · Share the dashboard

Pilots open http://<timer>:8088. RotorHazard's Settings → dd-pits panel links to the dashboard, the overlays and dd-pits' settings, behind your RotorHazard login.

Download the RotorHazard plugin Read the guide

Works with RotorHazard 4.0 and later. Heats, rounds, classes, laps and results come straight from the timer; the next heats show as "up next" with their lineups and channels.

Brackets

Finals that run themselves.

Pick a format, seed the pilots from qualifying, and dd-pits draws the races. Each race goes to RotorHazard or FPVTrackside the moment its line-up is known, and the results come back from the timing, so the next races fill in on their own.

Double elimination

Winners and losers brackets, finishing in a grand final with reset or a chase-the-ace series. Single elimination too.

MultiGP qualifiers

The top-4-advance sheet, and bracket progression: A, B, C brackets where the top finishers move up.

Ladders & points

Seeded ladders that climb to the top seed, and points brackets over several rounds with a points table you set.

Uses the same engine as the Stages in bob9's FPVTrackside, so the draw is identical. Pilots follow the bracket live on their phones; results can be entered or corrected by hand.

Race calls

One voice.
Right on the lap.

Laps, holeshots, results and "next up" — spoken by dd-pits instead of FPVTrackside's own speech, in your club's own wording.

Your club's wording

Calls are read from FPVTrackside's own Sounds.xml, so the phrasing and on/off switches you set there carry over. dd-pits finds FPVTrackside's files itself, and an audit flags settings that disagree.

One switch decides who speaks

dd-pits sets FPVTrackside's speech volume to match, so you never hear both — or neither. No voice set up? FPVTrackside keeps speaking and dd-pits stays out of the audio.

Fast calls

dd-pits listens for FPVTrackside's live events as well as polling it. Voice packs of pre-generated clips make common calls play instantly.

Race director controls

Hurry Up, Announce Race and the other calls FPVTrackside binds to keyboard shortcuts — trigger them from the laptop or any phone at the field.

Pronunciations

Tell the voice how to say a tricky callsign without changing how it's spelled anywhere else.

Clean hand-offs

When the operator moves to the next race, anything still waiting to be said about the last one is dropped.

Works with ELRS Netpack

One Ethernet cable.
Every goggle on the field.

ELRS Netpack is firmware for Ethernet ESP32 boards that acts as the ELRS timer backpack over the network. dd-pits talks to it over TCP — and it tells you whether each message actually reached the goggles.

BoardsWaveshare ESP32-S3-ETH · Olimex ESP32-POE-ISO
Power over EthernetOn a PoE board, one cable powers and connects it
Timer backpack over TCPdd-pits connects to elrs-netpack.local:8080
Delivery confirmationKnow which goggles got the message
Over-the-air updatesDrop new firmware on http://elrs-netpack.local/
Goggle test consoleOSD message, channel change, clock and DVR name — built in

ELRS Netpack was originally created by i-am-grub, and forked and extended by bob9. Not affiliated with the ExpressLRS project.

Questions.

Does it work with RotorHazard?

Yes. Install the dd-pits RotorHazard plugin (RotorHazard 4.0 or later) and the timer runs dd-pits itself, with the same dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggle OSD.

Does it replace FPVTrackside?

No. FPVTrackside keeps doing the timing. dd-pits reads it on the same laptop and does everything else: the dashboard, overlays, race calls, commentary and goggles.

Does it need the internet?

No. The dashboard, overlays, race calls, goggle OSD and API all run on the laptop. Only the cloud AI options (Gemini or Qwen) and YouTube Watch links use a connection — and the commentary booth can run fully locally on Apple Silicon.

How do pilots see the dashboard?

They open the laptop's address on their phones over the venue wifi. Settings pages only accept changes from the laptop itself, so a phone in the pits can't change anyone's goggle channel.

What does it cost?

Nothing. dd-pits is MIT licensed. If you use cloud AI for commentary, you pay your provider for what you use — and the /booth page shows the running spend.

Do I need an ELRS backpack?

Only for goggle features. A netpack on the network is fastest and confirms delivery; a USB backpack plugged into the laptop works too.

What if no voice is set up?

FPVTrackside keeps announcing exactly as it always has, and dd-pits stays out of the audio. You can never end up with silence by accident.
